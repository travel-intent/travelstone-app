What to eat in Chicago

Quick bite Chicago-style Hot Dog

An all-beef frank piled high with mustard, relish, onion, tomato, pickle, sport peppers, and celery salt—famously never with ketchup.

Main Deep-Dish Pizza

A knife-and-fork pie with a buttery crust, layers of gooey cheese, and chunky tomato sauce baked in a deep steel pan, pioneered by Pizzeria Uno.

Dessert Rainbow Cone

A towering five-flavor scoop of Chicago's beloved Rainbow Cone ice cream, layered with everything from Palmer House to pistachio.

Getting to Chicago

The main gateway is O'Hare International Airport (ORD). Arriving by train, the central station is Chicago Union Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Chicago

Headed to Chicago? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

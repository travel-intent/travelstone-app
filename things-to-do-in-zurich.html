What to eat in Zurich

A sizzling Swiss veal sausage tucked into a crusty bread roll, grabbed hot from a street stand near Bahnhofstrasse.

Main Zürcher Geschnetzeltes

Tender strips of veal in a creamy white wine and mushroom sauce, classically served with crispy Rösti.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Zurich

Headed to Zurich? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Swiss franc (CHF), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in CHF, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Switzerland. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Swiss franc in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Switzerland aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Nashville

Quick bite Nashville Hot Chicken

Fiery cayenne-crusted fried chicken served on white bread with pickles, a scorching legacy born at Prince's Hot Chicken Shack.

Main Meat and Three

A beloved Southern cafeteria-style plate where you pick one meat and three homestyle sides like mac and cheese, turnip greens, and cornbread.

Dessert Goo Goo Cluster

America's original candy bar cluster of caramel, marshmallow nougat, and peanuts blanketed in milk chocolate, a Nashville sweet since 1912.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Nashville

Headed to Nashville? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

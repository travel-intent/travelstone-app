What to eat in Hurghada

Quick bite Ta'ameya (Egyptian Fava Bean Falafel)

Crispy on the outside and herby-green inside, this Egyptian take on falafel made from fava beans is the ultimate grab-and-go bite along Hurghada's streets.

Main Grilled Red Sea Sayadeya (Fish with Spiced Rice)

Fresh catch straight from the Red Sea, pan-fried or grilled and served over fragrant cumin-spiced rice with caramelized onions—Hurghada's coastal specialty at its finest.

Dessert Om Ali

A warm, comforting Egyptian bread pudding soaked in sweetened milk and topped with nuts and raisins, the beloved finale to any Red Sea feast.

Don't miss in Hurghada

  • Red Sea scuba divingWorld-class coral reefs, wrecks, and crystal-clear visibility make Hurghada a top global diving destination.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Hurghada

Headed to Hurghada? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Egyptian pound (EGP), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EGP, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Egypt. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Egyptian pound in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Egypt aren't blocked as suspicious.

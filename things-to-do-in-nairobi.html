How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Nairobi

Headed to Nairobi? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Kenyan shilling (KES), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in KES, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Kenya. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Kenyan shilling in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Kenya aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Dubai

Quick bite Shawarma

Thinly shaved, marinated meat wrapped hot in flatbread with garlic sauce and pickles from a bustling Dubai stand.

Main Machboos

Emirati spiced rice piled with tender chicken or lamb, slow-cooked with dried lime and warm baharat spices.

Dessert Luqaimat

Golden, crispy-outside-fluffy-inside dumplings drizzled with date syrup or sweet sesame glaze.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Dubai

Headed to Dubai? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in UAE dirham (AED), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in AED, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United Arab Emirates. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little UAE dirham in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United Arab Emirates aren't blocked as suspicious.

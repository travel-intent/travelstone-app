What to eat in Manila

Quick bite Isaw

Smoky grilled chicken or pork intestines skewered and glazed, a beloved late-night street food obsession in Manila.

Try it at Lola Ote Restaurant
Main Crispy Pata

Deep-fried pork leg with shatteringly crisp skin and tender meat, a beloved centerpiece of Filipino feasts.

Try it at Gordo's Crispy Pata Pedro Gil Branch
Dessert Halo-Halo

A dazzling mound of shaved ice, evaporated milk, sweet beans, fruits, and ube ice cream that's pure Filipino summer joy.

Try it at Bebang Halo-Halo - Lucky Chinatown

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Manila

Headed to Manila? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Philippine peso (PHP), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in PHP, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Philippines. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Philippine peso in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Philippines aren't blocked as suspicious.

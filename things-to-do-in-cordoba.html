What to eat in Córdoba

Quick bite Salmorejo

A thick, chilled tomato and bread purée topped with hard-boiled egg and jamón, served in little cups as the ultimate refreshing Córdoba street bite.

Main Rabo de Toro

Tender oxtail slow-braised for hours in red wine until it falls off the bone — a rich, deeply savoury Córdoba classic born from its bullfighting heritage.

Dessert Pastel Cordobés

A flaky puff-pastry pie filled with sweet angel-hair pumpkin (cabello de ángel), dusted with sugar — a beloved local treat found in every pastry shop.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Córdoba

Headed to Córdoba? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Spain. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Spain aren't blocked as suspicious.

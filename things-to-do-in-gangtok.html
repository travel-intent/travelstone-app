What to eat in Gangtok

Quick bite Momos

Steaming hot Tibetan-style dumplings filled with pork, chicken, or vegetables, served with a fiery tomato-sesame chutney that Gangtok locals swear by.

Try it at Shuffle Momos (Delivery Only)
Main Gundruk-Sinki based Thali with Pork Sukuti

A hearty Sikkimese spread featuring fermented leafy greens (gundruk) and spicy dried pork (sukuti), showcasing the mountain kitchen's love for tangy, smoky flavors.

Dessert Sel Roti

A crispy, ring-shaped rice-flour doughnut, lightly sweetened and fried golden, traditionally enjoyed with a cup of hot butter tea.

Try it at Hamro Bhansa Ghar

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Gangtok

Headed to Gangtok? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Indian rupee (INR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in INR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in India. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Indian rupee in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in India aren't blocked as suspicious.

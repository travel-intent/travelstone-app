What to eat in Cape Town

Quick bite Gatsby

A massive, overstuffed sandwich loaded with fries, meat and spicy sauce that Cape Town locals swear by for a hearty, indulgent bite.

Main Cape Malay Curry

A fragrant, mildly sweet-spiced curry rooted in the city's Cape Malay heritage, often served with yellow rice and sambals.

Dessert Malva Pudding

A warm, spongy apricot-infused pudding drenched in a sweet caramel sauce that's a beloved staple of Cape comfort food.

Don't miss in Cape Town

  • Hike Table MountainTrek up one of the New7Wonders of Nature for sweeping views over the city and coastline.
  • Cage diving with great white sharksCome face-to-face with the ocean's apex predator in the waters off nearby Gansbaai or False Bay.
  • Whale watching along the Cape coastSpot southern right whales breaching just offshore during the winter and spring migration season.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Cape Town

Headed to Cape Town? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in South African rand (ZAR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in ZAR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in South Africa. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little South African rand in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in South Africa aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Marseille

Quick bite Panisse

Crispy golden chickpea-flour fritters, fried to a crunch and sprinkled with salt—a beloved Marseille street-side nibble.

Main Bouillabaisse

Marseille's legendary saffron-scented fish stew, simmered with local Mediterranean catch and served with rouille-slathered croutons.

Dessert Navette de Marseille

Slightly crunchy, orange-blossom-scented boat-shaped biscuits that have been baked in the city for centuries.

Don't miss in Marseille

  • Calanques boat tourCruise the turquoise fjord-like inlets of Calanques National Park, tucked between towering limestone cliffs.
  • Hiking the Calanques trailsTrek rugged coastal paths above hidden coves like Sormiou and En-Vau for dramatic Mediterranean views.
  • Snorkelling in the CalanquesDiscover the clear turquoise waters and marine life along this stunning limestone coastline.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Marseille

Headed to Marseille? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in France. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in France aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Stavanger

Quick bite Fiskepudding/Fiskeboller open sandwich from a Fiskebrygga stall

Steamed fish cakes and pudding served fresh by the harbor, a beloved everyday snack in this fishing city.

Main Stavanger-style Lapskaus

A hearty stew of tender meat, potatoes and root vegetables that has warmed local homes for generations.

Dessert Riskrem with red berry sauce

A creamy rice pudding folded with whipped cream and topped with tangy berry sauce, a festive Norwegian favorite adopted lovingly by Stavanger tables.

Don't miss in Stavanger

  • Hike to Pulpit Rock (Preikestolen)A steep but rewarding trek to a dramatic cliff plateau towering 600 metres above Lysefjord.
  • Lysefjord boat cruiseSail beneath towering cliffs and waterfalls for the classic view of Pulpit Rock from the water.

Getting to Stavanger

The main gateway is Stavanger Airport, Sola (SVG). Arriving by train, the central station is Stavanger Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Stavanger

Headed to Stavanger? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Norwegian krone (NOK), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in NOK, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Norway. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Norwegian krone in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Norway aren't blocked as suspicious.

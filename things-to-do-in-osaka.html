What to eat in Osaka

Quick bite Takoyaki

Piping-hot octopus-filled batter balls, crisped outside and molten within, drizzled with sauce, bonito flakes, and mayo—Osaka's street food icon.

Try it at 大阪名物大衆酒場 串カツとたこ焼きのらっしゃいセー 梅田 お初天神店
Main Okonomiyaki

Osaka's beloved savory pancake, layered with cabbage, pork, and a sweet-tangy sauce swirl, is the city's ultimate comfort food.

Try it at Umeda Okonomiyaki Izakaya Fuwatoro
Dessert Kuromon Ichiba Fruit Sando / Osaka-style Anmitsu

A cool, jewel-toned treat—think fresh fruit sandwiched in pillowy cream and milk bread, a sweet finish beloved by locals wandering the markets.

Try it at Kuromon Market

Getting to Osaka

The main gateway is Kansai International Airport (KIX). Arriving by train, the central station is Shin-Osaka Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Osaka

Headed to Osaka? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Japanese yen (JPY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in JPY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Japan. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Japanese yen in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Japan aren't blocked as suspicious.

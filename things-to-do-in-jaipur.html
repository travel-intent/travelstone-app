What to eat in Jaipur

Quick bite Pyaaz Kachori

Jaipur's beloved deep-fried, flaky pastry stuffed with a spicy onion filling, best eaten hot with tangy tamarind chutney from a street-side stall.

Try it at Puran Ji Kachori Wala
Main Laal Maas

A fiery, rich mutton curry simmered in a base of red Mathania chillies and yogurt, this Rajput royal classic is Jaipur's most celebrated main.

Try it at Laal Maas Restaurant
Dessert Ghewar

This disc-shaped, honeycombed sweet soaked in sugar syrup and topped with rabri is a Jaipur festive icon, especially during Teej.

Try it at Sambhar Fini Wala

Getting to Jaipur

The main gateway is Jaipur International Airport (JAI). Arriving by train, the central station is Jaipur Junction.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Jaipur

Headed to Jaipur? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Indian rupee (INR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in INR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in India. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Indian rupee in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in India aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in San Sebastián

Quick bite Gilda

The original pintxo—a punchy skewer of anchovy, olive, and guindilla pepper born in San Sebastián's Old Town bars.

Main Bacalao al Pil Pil

Silky salt cod bathed in an emulsified garlic-olive oil sauce, a Basque classic perfected in the city's txigorrería kitchens.

Dessert Tarta de Queso (La Viña style Basque Burnt Cheesecake)

The world-famous scorched-top, creamy-centered cheesecake invented right here at La Viña bar.

Don't miss in San Sebastián

  • Kayaking around La Concha BayPaddle across the shell-shaped bay toward Santa Clara Island with views of the city's elegant beachfront.
  • Hike Monte Igueldo or Monte UrgullClimb these coastal hills for sweeping panoramas over the bay and the Bay of Biscay.
  • Surfing and bodyboarding at Zurriola BeachRide Atlantic swells at one of mainland Europe's most famous urban surf breaks.

Getting to San Sebastián

The main gateway is San Sebastián Airport (EAS). Arriving by train, the central station is Donostia / San Sebastián.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in San Sebastián

Headed to San Sebastián? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Spain. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Spain aren't blocked as suspicious.

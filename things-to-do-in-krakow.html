What to eat in Kraków

Quick bite Obwarzanek Krakowski

A chewy, braided ring-shaped bread encrusted with poppy seeds or sesame, sold fresh from street carts all over the Old Town.

Main Pierogi (Kraków-style, often Ruskie)

Tender dumplings filled with potato, cheese and onion, pan-fried to golden perfection and served with a dollop of sour cream.

Dessert Rogal Świętomarciński (St. Martin's Croissant)

A flaky, crescent-shaped pastry stuffed with white poppy seed filling, nuts and dried fruit, traditionally devoured on November 11th.

Getting to Kraków

The main gateway is Kraków John Paul II Airport (KRK). Arriving by train, the central station is Kraków Główny.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Kraków

Headed to Kraków? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Polish złoty (PLN), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in PLN, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Poland. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Polish złoty in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Poland aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Durban

Quick bite Bunny Chow

A hollowed-out loaf of bread filled with fragrant curry, Durban's legendary street food invented by the city's Indian community.

Main Durban Curry (Mutton or Prawn)

Fiery, deeply spiced curry reflecting Durban's status as home to the largest Indian population outside India, best mopped up with roti.

Dessert Koeksisters

Syrup-soaked, plaited pastry fritters that turn sticky-sweet and crisp, a beloved treat across Durban's diverse communities.

Don't miss in Durban

  • Sardine Run boat toursWitness the ocean come alive each winter as massive sardine shoals draw dolphins, sharks, and whales close to Durban's coast.
  • Aliwal Shoal divingDive one of South Africa's top-rated reefs just south of Durban, famous for ragged-tooth sharks and vibrant soft corals.
  • uShaka Marine World & harbour boat tripsEnjoy a boat cruise around Durban's busy harbour before exploring Africa's largest marine theme park.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Durban

Headed to Durban? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in South African rand (ZAR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in ZAR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in South Africa. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little South African rand in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in South Africa aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Barcelona

Quick bite Pa amb tomàquet

Rustic bread rubbed with ripe tomato, olive oil and salt, the humble Catalan staple that starts every tapas spread.

Main Fideuà

Barcelona's answer to paella, this toasted noodle dish is packed with seafood and finished with a swirl of garlicky allioli.

Dessert Crema Catalana

A silky custard with a brittle caramelized sugar crust that cracks perfectly under your spoon.

Getting to Barcelona

The main gateway is Josep Tarradellas Barcelona–El Prat Airport (BCN). Arriving by train, the central station is Barcelona Sants.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Barcelona

Headed to Barcelona? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Spain. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Spain aren't blocked as suspicious.

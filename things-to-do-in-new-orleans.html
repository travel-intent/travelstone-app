What to eat in New Orleans

Quick bite Beignets

Pillowy squares of fried dough buried under a snowdrift of powdered sugar, best enjoyed hot at a bustling French Quarter café.

Main Gumbo

A deeply savory roux-based stew of okra, andouille sausage, and seafood that embodies New Orleans' Creole and Cajun soul in every spoonful.

Dessert Bananas Foster

A tableside spectacle of caramelized bananas flambéed in rum and brown sugar, invented in this very city and served warm over vanilla ice cream.

Getting to New Orleans

The main gateway is Louis Armstrong New Orleans International Airport (MSY). Arriving by train, the central station is New Orleans Union Passenger Terminal.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in New Orleans

Headed to New Orleans? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

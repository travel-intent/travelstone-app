What to eat in Valencia

Quick bite Bocadillo de Calamares

A crusty roll packed with crispy fried squid rings, a beloved grab-and-go bite from Valencia's bars and markets.

Main Paella Valenciana

The original rice masterpiece, simmered in a wide pan with rabbit, chicken, snails and saffron, born right in Valencia's fields.

Dessert Horchata de Chufa with Fartons

A chilled, nutty tiger-nut milk sipped alongside sweet, sugar-glazed pastry fingers made for dunking.

Getting to Valencia

The main gateway is Valencia Airport (VLC). Arriving by train, the central station is València Joaquín Sorolla.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Valencia

Headed to Valencia? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Spain. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Spain aren't blocked as suspicious.

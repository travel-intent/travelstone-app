What to eat in Miami

Quick bite Cuban Sandwich (Cubano)

A pressed, griddled masterpiece of ham, roast pork, Swiss cheese, pickles, and mustard on crusty Cuban bread that defines Miami's grab-and-go culture.

Try it at Sanguich
Main Ropa Vieja

Miami's beloved Cuban comfort food of slow-braised, shredded flank steak in a rich tomato-pepper sofrito, usually served with rice, black beans, and plantains.

Try it at Puerto Sagua Restaurant
Dessert Key Lime Pie

A tangy-sweet, custardy pie made from the tiny key limes of the Florida Keys, often piled high with meringue or whipped cream.

Try it at Fireman Derek’s Bake Shop

Getting to Miami

The main gateway is Miami International Airport (MIA). Arriving by train, the central station is MiamiCentral (Brightline).

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Miami

Headed to Miami? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Johannesburg

Quick bite Kota

A hollowed-out quarter loaf of bread stuffed with chips, cheese, polony, and atchar—Soweto's beloved messy, hearty street sandwich.

Main Braai (Boerewors & Pap)

Smoky, coiled boerewors sausage grilled over open flame and served with soft maize pap and a tangy tomato-onion relish—Joburg's social and culinary heartbeat.

Dessert Melktert

A silky, cinnamon-dusted custard tart with a delicate pastry shell—an Afrikaans classic every Johannesburg bakery takes pride in.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Johannesburg

Headed to Johannesburg? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in South African rand (ZAR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in ZAR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in South Africa. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little South African rand in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in South Africa aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Indore

Quick bite Garadu

Crispy fried purple yam chunks tossed with tangy masala and lemon, a beloved winter street snack found across Indore's bustling Sarafa and Chappan Dukan food streets.

Main Indori Poha-Jalebi

The city's iconic breakfast pairing of soft, tangy poha topped with sev and a side of crisp, syrupy jalebi that Indoris swear by to start the day.

Dessert Malpua with Rabri

Indore's beloved sweet pancake soaked in syrup and topped with thick, sweetened condensed milk, a dessert that ends any Sarafa Bazaar night on a high note.

Getting to Indore

The main gateway is Devi Ahilyabai Holkar Airport (IDR). Arriving by train, the central station is Indore Junction.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Indore

Headed to Indore? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Indian rupee (INR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in INR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in India. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Indian rupee in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in India aren't blocked as suspicious.

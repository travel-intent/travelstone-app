What to eat in Seoul

Quick bite Tteokbokki

Chewy rice cakes simmered in a fiery-sweet gochujang sauce, the ultimate Seoul street-food fix found at every pojangmacha stall.

Main Seoul-style Galbi (Grilled Marinated Short Ribs)

Sweet-savory soy-marinated beef ribs grilled tableside until smoky and caramelized, a centerpiece of Korean BBQ culture born in the capital.

Dessert Bingsu (Patbingsu)

A mountain of finely shaved ice topped with sweet red beans, condensed milk, and rice cakes, Seoul's beloved summer treat.

Getting to Seoul

The main gateway is Incheon International Airport (ICN). Arriving by train, the central station is Seoul Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Seoul

Headed to Seoul? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in South Korean won (KRW), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in KRW, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in South Korea. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little South Korean won in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in South Korea aren't blocked as suspicious.

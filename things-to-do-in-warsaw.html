What to eat in Warsaw

Quick bite Zapiekanka

A toasted half-baguette piled with mushrooms, cheese, and ketchup — Warsaw's beloved late-night street snack born at the Old Town's Plac Bankowy market.

Main Kotlet Schabowy

A golden, generously breaded pork cutlet served with boiled potatoes and tangy sauerkraut — the ultimate Polish home-cooked comfort food you'll find on every Warsaw menu.

Dessert Pączki

Deep-fried, plush doughnuts filled with rose-petal jam or custard and dusted with powdered sugar — Varsovians queue for these on Tłusty Czwartek (Fat Thursday).

Getting to Warsaw

The main gateway is Warsaw Chopin Airport (WAW). Arriving by train, the central station is Warszawa Centralna.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Warsaw

Headed to Warsaw? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Polish złoty (PLN), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in PLN, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Poland. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Polish złoty in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Poland aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Bordeaux

Quick bite Canelé

A small caramelized pastry with a crisp, dark crust hiding a soft custardy rum-and-vanilla center, best grabbed fresh from a Bordeaux bakery.

Main Entrecôte à la Bordelaise

A juicy grilled rib steak bathed in a rich red wine and shallot sauce, traditionally cooked over vine cuttings for a signature smoky depth.

Dessert Canelé de Bordeaux

Often enjoyed as a proper dessert with coffee, its caramelized shell and tender custard interior make it the city's most beloved sweet export.

Getting to Bordeaux

The main gateway is Bordeaux–Mérignac Airport (BOD).

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Bordeaux

Headed to Bordeaux? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in France. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in France aren't blocked as suspicious.

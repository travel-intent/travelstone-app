What to eat in Madrid

Quick bite Bocadillo de Calamares

A crusty baguette stuffed with crispy fried squid rings, best enjoyed standing at a bar near Plaza Mayor.

Try it at Restaurant La Campana
Main Cocido Madrileño

A hearty chickpea and meat stew served in stages, embodying the soul-warming comfort food that has fed Madrileños for generations.

Try it at LA BOLA
Dessert Churros con Chocolate

Crispy, golden fried dough sticks dunked into thick, velvety hot chocolate—an essential Madrid breakfast or late-night treat.

Try it at Chocolatería San Ginés

Getting to Madrid

The main gateway is Adolfo Suárez Madrid–Barajas Airport (MAD). Arriving by train, the central station is Madrid Puerta de Atocha.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Madrid

Headed to Madrid? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Spain. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Spain aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Kolkata

Quick bite Kathi Roll

Flaky paratha wrapped around smoky skewer-grilled kebabs and tangy onions, a Kolkata street food icon born on Park Street.

Main Kosha Mangsho

Slow-cooked mutton simmered in a rich, dark, spiced onion gravy until it turns melt-in-the-mouth tender—Bengali Sunday comfort at its finest.

Dessert Mishti Doi

Caramelized, fermented sweet yogurt set in earthen pots, offering a velvety, jaggery-kissed finish to any Bengali feast.

Getting to Kolkata

The main gateway is Netaji Subhas Chandra Bose International Airport (CCU). Arriving by train, the central station is Howrah Junction.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Kolkata

Headed to Kolkata? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Indian rupee (INR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in INR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in India. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Indian rupee in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in India aren't blocked as suspicious.

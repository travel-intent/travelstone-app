What to eat in Salzburg

Quick bite Bosna

Salzburg's own beloved street sausage snack—grilled bratwurst tucked in a bun with curry-spiced onions, mustard and parsley, invented right here in the 1950s.

Main Salzburger Schnitzel

A tender veal or pork schnitzel pan-fried in butter and topped with a rich ham-and-cheese-studded cream sauce, a hearty local classic.

Dessert Salzburger Nockerl

A gloriously fluffy, oven-baked soufflé of egg whites and sugar shaped like the city's hills, served warm and dusted with icing sugar.

Don't miss in Salzburg

  • Untersberg mountain hiking & cable carAscend this dramatic limestone massif on Salzburg's doorstep for sweeping alpine panoramas over the city and Alps.
  • Eisriesenwelt ice cave tourExplore the world's largest ice cave, a glittering underworld carved into the mountains just outside Salzburg.

Getting to Salzburg

The main gateway is Salzburg Airport (SZG). Arriving by train, the central station is Salzburg Hauptbahnhof.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Salzburg

Headed to Salzburg? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Austria. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Austria aren't blocked as suspicious.

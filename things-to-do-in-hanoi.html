What to eat in Hanoi

Quick bite Bánh Mì

A crispy French-inspired baguette packed with savory pâté, pickled veggies, and Vietnamese herbs for the perfect handheld bite.

Try it at Banh Mi Oi
Main Bún Chả

Smoky grilled pork patties served in a tangy fish-sauce broth with fresh herbs and rice noodles, a dish so beloved even Obama savored it here.

Try it at Bún chả ngon
Dessert Chè

A colorful sweet mixture of beans, jellies, and coconut milk that Hanoians enjoy chilled on a hot afternoon.

Try it at Chè Bốn Mùa

Getting to Hanoi

The main gateway is Noi Bai International Airport (HAN). Arriving by train, the central station is Hanoi Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Hanoi

Headed to Hanoi? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Vietnamese đồng (VND), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in VND, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Vietnam. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Vietnamese đồng in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Vietnam aren't blocked as suspicious.

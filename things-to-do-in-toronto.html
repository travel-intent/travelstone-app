What to eat in Toronto

Quick bite Peameal Bacon Sandwich

Thick-cut, cornmeal-crusted peameal bacon piled high on a soft bun at St. Lawrence Market is a true Toronto classic.

Main Toronto-style Butter Chicken

Toronto's massive South Asian community has made rich, creamy butter chicken a beloved staple across the city's countless curry houses.

Dessert Nanaimo Bar

This no-bake Canadian layered treat of chocolate, custard and coconut-crumb base is a cherished sweet found in bakeries across Toronto.

Getting to Toronto

The main gateway is Toronto Pearson International Airport (YYZ). Arriving by train, the central station is Union Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Toronto

Headed to Toronto? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Canadian dollar (CAD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in CAD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Canada. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Canadian dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Canada aren't blocked as suspicious.

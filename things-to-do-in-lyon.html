What to eat in Lyon

Quick bite Saucisson Brioché

Warm, savory sausage baked inside buttery brioche makes for the ultimate Lyonnais grab-and-go comfort food.

Main Quenelle de Brochet (sauce Nantua)

This pillowy pike dumpling bathed in creamy crayfish sauce is the crown jewel of Lyon's legendary bouchon cuisine.

Dessert Tarte aux Pralines

A stunning pink praline tart with a buttery crust, its sweet-crunchy filling made from candied almonds is pure Lyonnais indulgence.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Lyon

Headed to Lyon? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in France. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in France aren't blocked as suspicious.

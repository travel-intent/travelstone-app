What to eat in Málaga

Quick bite Boquerones en Vinagre / Fritos

Tiny local anchovies either marinated in vinegar and garlic or fried crisp, a beloved tapas bar staple sipped alongside a cold caña.

Main Espeto de Sardinas

Whole sardines skewered on cane sticks and slow-roasted over an open wood fire right on the beach, the ultimate symbol of Málaga's coastal cuisine.

Dessert Tarta Malagueña / Pasas al Ron con Helado

A sweet finish featuring Málaga's famous raisins soaked in sweet Moscatel wine, often paired with ice cream or almond cake.

Getting to Málaga

The main gateway is Málaga-Costa del Sol Airport (AGP).

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Málaga

Headed to Málaga? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Spain. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Spain aren't blocked as suspicious.

Wide, foldable slices with a thin, crisp-yet-chewy crust and tangy sauce that define the city's iconic street corners.

Getting to New York

The main gateway is John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK). Arriving by train, the central station is New York Penn Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in New York

Headed to New York? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Zadar

Quick bite Sardele na žaru (Zadar-style grilled sardines)

Fresh Adriatic sardines grilled simply with olive oil and lemon, sold and savored right by the harbor as the ultimate Dalmatian street bite.

Main Zadarski brudet

A rich, slow-simmered mixed fish and seafood stew in a tomato-wine sauce, served over creamy polenta and cherished as Zadar's coastal soul food.

Dessert Zadarska torta (Zadar cake with maraschino liqueur)

A delicate almond and lemon cake infused with the city's famous maraschino cherry liqueur, a sweet symbol of Zadar's heritage.

Don't miss in Zadar

  • Kornati Islands boat tourSail through the stark, otherworldly archipelago of over 140 islands scattered off Zadar's coast.
  • Sunset at the Sea OrganWatch the Adriatic sunset while the waves play haunting music through Zadar's famous sea organ, often paired with a boat cruise along the coast.
  • Adriatic coast snorkelling tripsExplore the clear turquoise waters and hidden coves around Zadar's islands on a snorkelling excursion.

Getting to Zadar

The main gateway is Zadar Airport (ZAD). Arriving by train, the central station is Zadar Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Zadar

Headed to Zadar? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Croatia. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Croatia aren't blocked as suspicious.

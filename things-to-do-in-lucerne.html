What to eat in Lucerne

Quick bite Rösti

Crispy golden shredded-potato cakes, a Swiss staple you'll find perfected in cozy Lucerne taverns.

Main Chügelipastete

A delicate puff-pastry vol-au-vent filled with veal, mushrooms and cream sauce, a proud Lucerne classic served at festive tables.

Dessert Luzerner Lebkuchen

A spiced honey-and-almond gingerbread that's become the city's beloved edible souvenir.

Don't miss in Lucerne

  • Lake Lucerne paddle-steamer cruiseGlide across turquoise waters on a historic paddle steamer with views of snow-capped peaks ringing the lake.
  • Mount Pilatus hike and cogwheel railwayTackle trails on the legendary 'Dragon Mountain' then descend via the world's steepest cogwheel railway.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Lucerne

Headed to Lucerne? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Swiss franc (CHF), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in CHF, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Switzerland. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Swiss franc in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Switzerland aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Florence

Quick bite Lampredotto

Florence's beloved street sandwich made from slow-simmered cow's stomach, piled into a crusty roll and topped with salsa verde or spicy oil.

Main Bistecca alla Fiorentina

A towering, bone-in T-bone steak from Chianina cattle, grilled rare over an open flame and seasoned simply with olive oil, salt and pepper.

Dessert Schiacciata alla Fiorentina

A soft, orange-scented sponge cake dusted with powdered sugar, traditionally enjoyed during Carnevale season.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Florence

Headed to Florence? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Italy. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Italy aren't blocked as suspicious.

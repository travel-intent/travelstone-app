What to eat in Jeju

Quick bite Heuk Dwaeji Kkochi (Jeju Black Pork Skewers)

Smoky, juicy skewers of Jeju's famous black pig grilled at street stalls until irresistibly charred.

Main Heuk Dwaeji Gui (Jeju Black Pork Barbecue)

Thick-cut slices of tender, marbled black pork grilled tableside and dipped in fermented shrimp sauce, a Jeju point of pride.

Dessert Hallabong Ice Cream / Hallabong-based sweets

A refreshingly citrusy treat made from Jeju's beloved sweet-tart hallabong tangerines.

Don't miss in Jeju

  • Sunrise hike up Seongsan IlchulbongClimb this UNESCO-listed tuff cone crater to watch the sun rise over the East Sea.
  • Haenyeo-style diving off Jeju's coastExplore volcanic reefs and lava tubes made famous by the island's legendary free-diving sea women.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Jeju

Headed to Jeju? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in South Korean won (KRW), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in KRW, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in South Korea. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little South Korean won in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in South Korea aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Vancouver

Quick bite Japadog

Vancouver's beloved street cart mashup of a hot dog topped with Japanese flavors like teriyaki and mayo that locals line up for.

Main Spot Prawn Dish (West Coast Spot Prawns)

Sweet, briny wild-caught spot prawns, often simply seared with garlic and butter, celebrated every spring as a Pacific Northwest delicacy.

Dessert Nanaimo Bar

A no-bake layered treat of chocolate, custard, and coconut-crumb base that hails from nearby Nanaimo but is a proud BC classic sold across Vancouver.

Don't miss in Vancouver

  • Whale watching in the Salish SeaSpot orcas and humpback whales on a boat tour departing from Vancouver's waterfront.
  • Grouse Grind hikeTackle Vancouver's famous 'Mother Nature's Stairmaster' up Grouse Mountain for sweeping city and ocean views.
  • Kayaking around Stanley Park & False CreekPaddle past seawalls, forested shoreline, and the city skyline on the surrounding waters.

Getting to Vancouver

The main gateway is Vancouver International Airport (YVR). Arriving by train, the central station is Pacific Central Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Vancouver

Headed to Vancouver? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Canadian dollar (CAD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in CAD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Canada. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Canadian dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Canada aren't blocked as suspicious.

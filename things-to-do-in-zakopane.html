What to eat in Zakopane

Quick bite Grilled Oscypek

This smoked, salty sheep's milk cheese, grilled over coals and served with lingonberry jam, is the unmistakable taste of Zakopane's street stalls.

Main Kwaśnica

This hearty sauerkraut and smoked pork soup, simmered low and slow, is the ultimate warming bowl after a day in the Tatra Mountains.

Dessert Kremówka Zakopiańska / Oscypek na ciepło with cranberry

A warm slab of smoky grilled oscypek drizzled with tart cranberry sauce is the mountain-town treat locals can't resist as a sweet-savoury finish.

Don't miss in Zakopane

  • Trekking in the Tatra MountainsZakopane is the gateway to Poland's High Tatras, with iconic trails like Morskie Oko and Giewont drawing hikers year-round.
  • Exploring the Tatra limestone cavesNearby caves such as Mroźna and Mylna offer a cool underground adventure through dramatic rock formations.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Zakopane

Headed to Zakopane? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Polish złoty (PLN), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in PLN, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Poland. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Polish złoty in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Poland aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Tauranga

Quick bite Hangi-style Hokai Bites

Traditional Māori hāngī-cooked morsels of meat and kumara, earthy and smoky from being steamed underground, offer a quick taste of local Māori heritage.

Main Fresh Catch Fish and Chips (Snapper)

Straight off the boats of Tauranga Harbour, locally caught snapper battered and fried is a beloved waterfront staple best eaten looking out over the marina.

Dessert Pavlova with Bay of Plenty kiwifruit

This meringue-based classic gets a local twist topped with sun-ripened Bay of Plenty kiwifruit, the region's famous golden and green harvest.

Don't miss in Tauranga

  • White Island (Whakaari) volcano boat tourCruise out to New Zealand's most active marine volcano for an otherworldly up-close look at steaming craters and sulphur vents.
  • Mount Maunganui summit walkClimb the iconic volcanic cone overlooking golden beaches for sweeping Bay of Plenty views.
  • Reef and marine life trips around Mayor Island (Tuhua)Explore clear coastal waters and volcanic reefs teeming with fish just off the Tauranga coast.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Tauranga

Headed to Tauranga? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in New Zealand dollar (NZD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in NZD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in New Zealand. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little New Zealand dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in New Zealand aren't blocked as suspicious.

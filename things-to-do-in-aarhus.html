What to eat in Aarhus

Quick bite Flæskesteg sandwich

Crispy-skinned roast pork stuffed into a soft bun with red cabbage and pickles, a hearty Danish street favorite you'll find at Aarhus's food stalls.

Main Stjerneskud (Shooting Star)

A classic Danish smørrebrød piled high with both steamed and fried plaice, shrimp, and caviar on buttered rye bread, adored across Aarhus's lunch spots.

Dessert Æbleskiver

Fluffy round pancake balls dusted with powdered sugar and served with jam, a beloved treat especially around Danish Christmas markets.

Getting to Aarhus

The main gateway is Aarhus Airport (AAR). Arriving by train, the central station is Aarhus H.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Aarhus

Headed to Aarhus? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Danish krone (DKK), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in DKK, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Denmark. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Danish krone in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Denmark aren't blocked as suspicious.

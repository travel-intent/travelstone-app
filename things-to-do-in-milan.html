What to eat in Milan

Quick bite Panzerotto

This pocket of fried or baked dough oozing with tomato and mozzarella is the beloved grab-and-go snack of Milan's streets.

Main Cotoletta alla Milanese

A golden, bone-in veal cutlet breaded and pan-fried in butter until irresistibly crisp is the pride of Milanese kitchens.

Dessert Panettone

This tall, airy sweet bread studded with candied fruit and raisins is Milan's gift to Christmas tables worldwide.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Milan

Headed to Milan? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Italy. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Italy aren't blocked as suspicious.

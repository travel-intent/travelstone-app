What to eat in Honolulu

Quick bite Poke Bowl

Fresh cubes of ahi tuna marinated in shoyu, sesame oil, and green onion, served over rice for the ultimate island grab-and-go.

Main Loco Moco

A hearty Hawaiian comfort classic of rice topped with a juicy beef patty, fried egg, and rich brown gravy.

Dessert Shave Ice

Impossibly fine, fluffy ice drenched in vibrant tropical syrups, often crowned with sweet azuki beans or condensed milk.

  • Diamond Head Crater HikeClimb to the summit of this famous volcanic tuff cone for sweeping views of Waikiki and the Pacific.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Honolulu

Headed to Honolulu? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

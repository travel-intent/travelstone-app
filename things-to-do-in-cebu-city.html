What to eat in Cebu City

Quick bite Cebu Ngohiong

A deep-fried, five-spice-scented spring roll of singkamas and pork, dipped in sweet-tangy sauce for the ultimate street-side crunch.

Try it at Chinese Ngohiong
Main Cebu Lechon

Whole roasted pig with crackling-crisp skin and herb-stuffed, smoky meat so good Anthony Bourdain called it the best pig ever.

Try it at House of Lechon
Dessert Dried Mangoes with Pastillas or Cebu's Dried Mango

Cebu's world-famous sun-ripened dried mangoes offer a chewy, tangy-sweet bite that's become the island's edible souvenir.

Try it at Guadalupe Dried Mangoes

Don't miss in Cebu City

  • Whale shark encounter in OslobSwim or snorkel alongside gentle whale sharks in the shallow waters of Oslob, a short trip from Cebu City.
  • Moalboal sardine run and reef divingDive into a swirling tornado of millions of sardines just off Moalboal's coast, a world-famous underwater spectacle.
  • Kawasan Falls canyoneeringTrek, jump, and swim through turquoise cascades and canyons culminating in the iconic multi-tiered Kawasan Falls.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Cebu City

Headed to Cebu City? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Philippine peso (PHP), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in PHP, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Philippines. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Philippine peso in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Philippines aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Phuket

Quick bite Hokkien Mee (Phuket-style)

This Sino-Portuguese-influenced stir-fried yellow noodle dish, loaded with pork, seafood, and a rich dark sauce, is the taste of Phuket Town's street corners.

Try it at Go La Hokkien Noodles
Main Moo Hong

Phuket's soy-braised, sweet-savoury pork belly stew, slow-cooked until melt-in-your-mouth tender, is the island's ultimate comfort dish.

Try it at Raya Restaurant
Dessert O Aew

This Phuket-style dessert of shaved ice, chewy fig-jelly, and syrup with peanuts is a local secret weapon against the tropical heat.

Try it at โอ้เอ๋ว ถนนดีบุก

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Phuket

Headed to Phuket? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Thai baht (THB), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in THB, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Thailand. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Thai baht in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Thailand aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Washington

Quick bite Half-Smoke (Ben's Chili Bowl style)

A DC original: a smoky, spicy half-pork half-beef sausage piled with mustard and chili, best eaten late-night at a classic counter.

Main Chesapeake Blue Crab (Maryland-style Crab Cakes/Steamed Crabs)

Sweet, lump blue crab from the nearby Chesapeake Bay, steamed with Old Bay or formed into golden crab cakes, is the region's pride.

Dessert Chocolate Chip Cookie / Half-and-Half Pie from Eastern Market bakeries

Actually, the true DC sweet icon is the Moon Pie-style 'DC Half Smoke' — no: the classic is simply a rich Chesapeake-style Smith Island Cake slice, layered thin with fudgy frosting.

Getting to Washington

The main gateway is Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport (DCA). Arriving by train, the central station is Washington Union Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Washington

Headed to Washington? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

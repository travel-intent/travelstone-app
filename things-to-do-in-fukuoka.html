What to eat in Fukuoka

Quick bite Yatai Hakata Ramen

Slurp rich, milky tonkotsu ramen with thin noodles from a steamy riverside street stall, the way Fukuoka locals have done for generations.

Main Motsunabe

This bubbling hot pot of tender beef or pork offal simmered with chives, garlic and cabbage in a savory miso or soy broth is Fukuoka comfort food at its heartiest.

Dessert Hakata Torimon

A beloved Fukuoka confection of soft, chewy mochi wrapped around sweet white bean paste, offering a delicate bite locals proudly gift and savor.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Fukuoka

Headed to Fukuoka? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Japanese yen (JPY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in JPY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Japan. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Japanese yen in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Japan aren't blocked as suspicious.

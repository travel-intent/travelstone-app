What to eat in Seattle

Quick bite Pike Place Market Fish Toss Salmon Fish and Chips

Crispy, flaky salmon fish and chips devoured fresh by the waterfront amid the famous fish-throwing hawkers of Pike Place Market.

Main Wild Alaskan/Pacific Salmon (Cedar Plank Grilled)

Buttery, smoky cedar-plank salmon celebrates the Pacific Northwest's deep-rooted salmon culture and pristine coastal waters.

Dessert Frango Mints Chocolate (or Seattle-style Gelato from Fainting Goat/Molly Moon's Ice Cream)

Molly Moon's honey-lavender ice cream is a beloved local scoop that captures Seattle's love for creative, small-batch sweets.

Getting to Seattle

The main gateway is Seattle–Tacoma International Airport (SEA). Arriving by train, the central station is King Street Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Seattle

Headed to Seattle? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in US dollar (USD), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in USD, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United States. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little US dollar in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United States aren't blocked as suspicious.

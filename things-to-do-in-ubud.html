What to eat in Ubud

Quick bite Sate Lilit

Minced spiced fish or chicken satay pressed onto lemongrass stalks and grilled over coconut husk charcoal for a smoky bite.

Main Bebek Betutu

Balinese duck slow-cooked for hours in banana leaves with a fragrant spice paste until impossibly tender and aromatic.

Dessert Klepon

Sweet pandan rice cakes that burst with molten palm sugar and are rolled in fresh grated coconut.

Don't miss in Ubud

  • Sunrise trek up Mount BaturHike through the dark to summit an active volcano and watch the sunrise over Bali's highlands.
  • Ayung River white-water raftingPaddle through lush jungle gorges and past waterfalls on Bali's most famous rafting river.
  • Goa Gajah and rice terrace trekkingExplore ancient elephant caves and wander the iconic terraced rice paddies surrounding Ubud.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Ubud

Headed to Ubud? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Indonesian rupiah (IDR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in IDR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Indonesia. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Indonesian rupiah in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Indonesia aren't blocked as suspicious.

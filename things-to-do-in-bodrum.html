What to eat in Bodrum

Quick bite Bodrum Kumru

A crusty sesame-studded roll stuffed with sujuk, cheese, tomatoes and peppers, then pressed until melty—Bodrum's beloved sandwich obsession.

Main Bodrum Balığı (Fresh Grilled Fish, e.g. Çipura/Levrek)

Just-caught sea bream or sea bass grilled simply with olive oil and lemon, served harbourside as the Aegean coast's ultimate catch-of-the-day.

Dessert Bodrum Dondurması (Maraş-style Ice Cream with local twists)

Thick, chewy, hand-pounded ice cream often studded with pistachios—an irresistible sweet treat while strolling the marina.

Don't miss in Bodrum

  • Bodrum Gulet CruiseSail the turquoise Aegean coves aboard a traditional wooden gulet, stopping for swims in hidden bays.
  • Aegean Scuba DivingExplore submerged ruins and vibrant reefs in Bodrum's clear coastal waters, a hub for Turkey's diving scene.
  • Coastal Cove SnorkellingDiscover rocky inlets and crystal-clear water teeming with fish along the Bodrum peninsula.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Bodrum

Headed to Bodrum? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Turkish lira (TRY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in TRY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Turkey. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Turkish lira in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Turkey aren't blocked as suspicious.

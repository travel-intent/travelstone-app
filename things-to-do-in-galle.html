What to eat in Galle

Quick bite Fish Cutlets (Sri Lankan style)

Crispy, deep-fried spiced fish and potato balls sold hot from Galle Fort's street corners and bakeries, perfect with a squeeze of lime.

Try it at Nisala Food
Main Galle-style Fish Ambul Thiyal

Chunks of firm fish like tuna slow-cooked dry in a tangy, dark goraka (garcinia) and pepper sauce, a coastal Southern classic.

Try it at Bastille Fort Galle
Dessert Wattalapam

A rich, spiced coconut custard sweetened with jaggery, showing off Galle's deep Malay and Moorish heritage.

Try it at Akeera's Watalappam

Don't miss in Galle

  • Snorkelling the reefs off UnawatunaJust outside Galle, calm turquoise bays teem with reef fish and coral gardens perfect for snorkelling.
  • Whale watching from nearby MirissaDay trips from Galle head to Mirissa's deep waters, among the world's best spots to see blue whales and sperm whales.
  • Stilt fishermen boat excursionsTake a boat or coastal outing to watch Sri Lanka's iconic stilt fishermen perched over the surf near Galle's shoreline.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Galle

Headed to Galle? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Sri Lankan rupee (LKR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in LKR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Sri Lanka. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Sri Lankan rupee in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Sri Lanka aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Nagoya

Quick bite Tebasaki

Crispy double-fried chicken wings glazed in a sweet-savory soy sauce and pepper coating, a Nagoya izakaya favorite locals can't get enough of.

Main Hitsumabushi

Nagoya's prized way to eat grilled eel over rice, savored in three delicious stages—plain, with condiments, and finished with savory dashi broth.

Dessert Uiro

A subtly sweet, chewy rice-flour confection that's been a beloved Nagoya teahouse treat for centuries.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Nagoya

Headed to Nagoya? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Japanese yen (JPY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in JPY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Japan. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Japanese yen in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Japan aren't blocked as suspicious.

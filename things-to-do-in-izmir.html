What to eat in Izmir

Quick bite Izmir Kumru

A toasted sesame-crusted roll stuffed with sujuk, sucuk sausage, salami, tomatoes, and melted cheese, sold at beachside stalls all over the city.

Main Izmir Köfte

Juicy meatballs simmered in a rich tomato sauce with potatoes, a homely classic that locals swear by.

Dessert Boyoz

A flaky, sesame-free pastry of Sephardic Jewish origin, traditionally enjoyed warm with a boiled egg and a glass of tea for breakfast.

Getting to Izmir

The main gateway is İzmir Adnan Menderes Airport (ADB). Arriving by train, the central station is İzmir Basmane Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Izmir

Headed to Izmir? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Turkish lira (TRY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in TRY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Turkey. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Turkish lira in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Turkey aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Nara

Quick bite Nara-zuke

Crunchy vegetables pickled in fragrant sake lees, a pungent and addictive local snack you'll find in every Nara market.

Main Kakinoha-zushi

Vinegared mackerel or salmon sushi pressed and wrapped in fragrant persimmon leaves, a centuries-old Nara specialty bursting with delicate, cured flavor.

Dessert Kuzu-mochi (or Yomogi mochi)

Delicately chewy mochi made with local kudzu starch or mugwort, offering a subtly sweet, refreshing finish to a Nara meal.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Nara

Headed to Nara? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Japanese yen (JPY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in JPY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Japan. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Japanese yen in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Japan aren't blocked as suspicious.

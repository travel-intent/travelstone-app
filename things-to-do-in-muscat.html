What to eat in Muscat

Quick bite Omani Mashuai (grilled kingfish with lime rice)

Fresh kingfish grilled whole over open flame and served with tangy lime-infused rice, a beloved coastal snack-turned-meal in Muscat's harbor stalls.

Main Shuwa

Tender, marinated lamb or goat wrapped in banana leaves and slow-cooked in an underground sand oven for a whole day, prized for special occasions.

Dessert Omani Halwa

A rich, glossy, spiced sweet slow-cooked in giant copper pots until it turns deep amber and irresistibly sticky.

Don't miss in Muscat

  • Diving the Daymaniyat IslandsVibrant coral reefs and turtles just a boat ride from Muscat's coastline.
  • Dolphin and whale watching cruiseSpot spinner dolphins leaping in the waters off Muscat's dramatic coast.
  • Trekking the Western Hajar MountainsRugged canyon trails like Wadi Shab and Snake Canyon just outside the city.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Muscat

Headed to Muscat? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Omani rial (OMR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in OMR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Oman. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Omani rial in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Oman aren't blocked as suspicious.

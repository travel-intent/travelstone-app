What to eat in Kanazawa

Quick bite Kaisen-don at Omicho Market

A bowl piled high with glistening fresh sashimi from the Sea of Japan, assembled right before your eyes at Kanazawa's famed 'kitchen' market.

Main Nodoguro (Kanazawa-style grilled blackthroat seafood)

Prized nodoguro from the Sea of Japan is grilled or served as sushi at Omicho Market, its rich, buttery flesh melting on the tongue.

Dessert Kanazawa Gold Leaf Ice Cream (Kinpaku Soft Cream)

A soft-serve cone delicately wrapped in a shimmering sheet of edible 24-karat gold leaf, a dazzling nod to Kanazawa's centuries-old gold-leaf craft.

Getting to Kanazawa

The main gateway is Komatsu Airport (KMQ). Arriving by train, the central station is Kanazawa Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Kanazawa

Headed to Kanazawa? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Japanese yen (JPY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in JPY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Japan. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Japanese yen in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Japan aren't blocked as suspicious.

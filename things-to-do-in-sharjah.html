What to eat in Sharjah

Quick bite Luqaimat

Golden, crispy-edged dumplings drizzled with date syrup or honey, sold hot at Sharjah's souks and corniche stalls.

Main Machboos

Fragrant spiced rice slow-cooked with tender chicken or lamb and dried lime, the Emirati comfort dish locals swear by.

Dessert Khabees

A sticky, aromatic date-and-saffron pudding-like sweet that's a beloved finish to any Sharjah family feast.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Sharjah

Headed to Sharjah? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in UAE dirham (AED), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in AED, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in United Arab Emirates. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little UAE dirham in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in United Arab Emirates aren't blocked as suspicious.

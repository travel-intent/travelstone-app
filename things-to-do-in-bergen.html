What to eat in Bergen

Quick bite Skillingsboller

Bergen's famously oversized, gooey cinnamon buns are a beloved local snack, best enjoyed warm from a bakery in the old town.

Main Bergen Fiskesuppe

This creamy, delicately spiced fish soup, packed with fresh catch from the fjords, is the city's edible pride and joy.

Dessert Skolebrød

A classic Norwegian sweet bun filled with custard and topped with coconut icing, cherished as an afternoon treat in Bergen's cafés.

Don't miss in Bergen

  • Hike up Mount Fløyen or UlrikenTrek (or take the funicular/cable car) to panoramic viewpoints over Bergen's fjord-ringed cityscape.
  • Norwegian fjord cruise from BergenBergen is the classic gateway for scenic boat trips into the Nærøyfjord and Hardangerfjord, among the world's most dramatic fjords.
  • Mostraumen fjord safari by RIB boatSpeed through narrow fjord arms and past waterfalls on an exhilarating small-boat excursion from the city harbor.

Getting to Bergen

The main gateway is Bergen Airport, Flesland (BGO). Arriving by train, the central station is Bergen Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Bergen

Headed to Bergen? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Norwegian krone (NOK), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in NOK, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Norway. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Norwegian krone in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Norway aren't blocked as suspicious.

What to eat in Ankara

Quick bite Simit

A crispy, sesame-crusted ring of bread sold fresh on nearly every Ankara street corner, perfect for an on-the-go bite with tea.

Main Ankara Tava (Ankara Tavası)

A hearty, rice-topped lamb dish slow-cooked with the region's own prized meat, embodying the pride of Ankara's culinary heritage.

Dessert Güllaç

A delicate rosewater-scented milk and pastry dessert traditionally savored during Ramadan, especially cherished in Central Anatolia.

Getting to Ankara

The main gateway is Esenboğa International Airport (ESB).

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Ankara

Headed to Ankara? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Turkish lira (TRY), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in TRY, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Turkey. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Turkish lira in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Turkey aren't blocked as suspicious.

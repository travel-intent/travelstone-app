What to eat in Oslo

Quick bite Pølse i lompe

A steaming Norwegian hot dog wrapped in a soft potato flatbread, grabbed from street carts all across Oslo.

Main Fårikål

Norway's national dish—a comforting stew of mutton and cabbage slow-simmered with whole peppercorns, deeply tied to Oslo's autumn traditions.

Dessert Krumkake

A delicate, cone-shaped waffle cookie often filled with whipped cream, cherished as a beloved Norwegian holiday treat.

Don't miss in Oslo

  • Oslomarka forest and lake hikingVast network of forested trails and lakes right at the city's edge, perfect for a day of fresh-air hiking.
  • Oslofjord boat cruiseSail among the islands and skerries of the Oslofjord for classic views of the city skyline from the water.

Getting to Oslo

The main gateway is Oslo Airport, Gardermoen (OSL). Arriving by train, the central station is Oslo Central Station.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Oslo

Headed to Oslo? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in Norwegian krone (NOK), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in NOK, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Norway. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little Norwegian krone in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Norway aren't blocked as suspicious.

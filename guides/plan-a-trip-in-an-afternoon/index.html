Planning

Plan a trip in an afternoon

The hardest part of any trip is the blank page. Here's a calm, repeatable way to get from "we're going to Lisbon" to a real day-by-day plan — without twelve open tabs.

1. Set your anchors first

Before filling any days, pin the fixed points: arrival and departure times, anything already booked, and the one or two things you'd be sad to miss. Everything else arranges itself around these anchors. A trip with three good anchors practically plans itself; a trip with none sprawls.

2. Cluster by area, not by wish-list

Group the places you want to see by neighbourhood, then give each cluster a day. This one move saves more time on the ground than any other — you walk a compact loop instead of criss-crossing the city, and you naturally stumble on the good streets in between. Aim for one main area per day.

3. Leave real slack

The most common planning mistake is packing the day wall-to-wall. Plan for roughly two-thirds of the hours you have; the rest goes to long lunches, a museum that ran over, or simply sitting somewhere nice. A plan with slack survives contact with a real day. A plan without it becomes a to-do list you resent.

4. Sequence for energy and opening times

Put the big, timed or crowd-sensitive things early — landmarks before the queues build, markets while they're busy. Keep the afternoon looser and the evening for food. Check a couple of opening days so you don't arrive on the one day the place is shut.

5. Keep it changeable

A plan is a starting point, not a contract. The best itinerary is one you can rearrange when it rains, when you're tired, or when a local tips you off about somewhere better. Book the fixed things flexibly where you can, and treat the rest as pencil, not ink.

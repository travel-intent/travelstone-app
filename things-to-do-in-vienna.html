What to eat in Vienna

Quick bite Käsekrainer

A juicy cheese-studded sausage grabbed hot from a Würstelstand cart on a Vienna street corner.

Main Wiener Schnitzel

A golden, paper-thin breaded veal cutlet fried to crackling perfection, the pride of every Viennese Beisl.

Dessert Sachertorte

A dense chocolate cake layered with apricot jam and glazed in dark chocolate, born in Vienna's grand Hotel Sacher.

Getting to Vienna

The main gateway is Vienna International Airport (VIE). Arriving by train, the central station is Wien Hauptbahnhof.

How to avoid foreign transaction fees in Vienna

Headed to Vienna? Here's how to avoid foreign transaction fees (also called international transaction fees) on the trip. You'll pay in euro (EUR), and while cards work almost everywhere, many home-country cards quietly add about 1–3% to every purchase abroad. Four ways to keep that money:

  • Always pay in EUR, never your home currency. If a card terminal offers to charge you in your own currency ("dynamic currency conversion"), decline — that built-in rate is usually worse than your bank's.
  • Use a card with no foreign transaction fee in Austria. Many travel and digital-bank cards waive the 1–3% charge entirely.
  • Withdraw and carry a little euro in cash for markets, small vendors and tips, and skip ATMs that push a conversion on you.
  • Tell your bank your travel dates so your payments in Austria aren't blocked as suspicious.
